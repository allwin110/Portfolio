# Background

The Cloud Orchestration platform was designed to simplify infrastructure provisioning, orchestration management, and operational visibility for enterprise IT administrators and infrastructure teams.

The product aimed to centralize complex infrastructure workflows such as provisioning, deployment monitoring, workload orchestration, RBAC management, cluster visibility, and operational lifecycle management into a unified enterprise platform.

The primary objective was to reduce operational friction for infrastructure teams managing large-scale cloud environments while improving scalability, workflow clarity, and deployment efficiency.

---

# Initial Problems

Before the UX improvements, infrastructure workflows were highly fragmented and operationally complex.

Users had to navigate:
- deeply nested configuration structures,
- inconsistent workflow sequences,
- unclear infrastructure states,
- overloaded admin interfaces,
- complex RBAC hierarchy management,
- difficult provisioning flows.

Operational visibility was limited, making it difficult for users to quickly understand deployment states, orchestration progress, or tenant-level infrastructure conditions.

The lack of workflow clarity increased onboarding difficulty for new users and slowed operational efficiency for experienced administrators.

---

# User Complexity

The platform served highly technical enterprise users including:
- infrastructure administrators,
- DevOps engineers,
- platform operators,
- enterprise IT teams.

These users operated in environments involving:
- multi-tenant orchestration,
- infrastructure provisioning,
- workload management,
- Kubernetes-based deployments,
- role-based access management,
- operational monitoring.

The workflows were difficult because users needed to manage multiple infrastructure layers simultaneously while maintaining awareness of system states, deployment dependencies, and permission hierarchies.

Large operational tables, configuration-heavy interfaces, and high-density information increased cognitive load during critical workflows.

# UX Strategy

The UX strategy focused on reducing operational complexity without removing necessary enterprise functionality.

Key UX decisions included:
- progressive disclosure for configuration-heavy workflows,
- reducing unnecessary hierarchy exposure,
- improving infrastructure state visibility,
- simplifying RBAC navigation,
- restructuring provisioning sequences,
- improving admin workflow clarity,
- optimizing large enterprise data tables,
- improving operational scanning behavior.

The strategy emphasized scalable enterprise usability rather than visual simplification alone.
